Chart guide & level references

Charts use separate labeled scales for each unit. Solid lines show bucket averages; dashed lines show peaks. Hover, tap, or use arrow keys to inspect readings. Green OK and orange Warning compare each reading with its named reference; warnings show the excess in the measurement’s units. The next row shows reading ÷ reference × 100; percentages above 100% are bold red. A higher peak takes priority and is labeled Peak. These comparisons do not establish safe exposure.

CO₂ · ventilationAbove 1,000 ppm is our early reminder; above 1,500 ppm, improve ventilation if readings persist. Red indicators start at 2,000 ppm as a stronger reminder. These are ventilation indicators, not toxicity limits. HSE guidance ↗

Particles · daily exposureWHO’s 24-hour references are 15 µg/m³ for PM2.5 and 45 µg/m³ for PM10. Card indicators use a time-weighted daily average once at least 90% of the day is covered. Chart lines are daily references, not instant danger thresholds. WHO guidelines ↗

VOC / NOₓ · relative changesVOC’s learned baseline is 100; NOₓ’s is 1. Colors follow Sensirion’s air-purifier response bands. These indexes do not measure a gas concentration or establish toxic exposure. Zero or missing readings mean unavailable. Sensirion index guide ↗

Humidity warnings use EPA’s below-60% ceiling and 30% ideal minimum; the ideal range is 30–50%. Differences use percentage points (pp). EPA humidity guidance ↗ Temperature, PM1, and PM4.0 have no applied limit and stay neutral.

This monitor cannot establish that printer emissions are safe: it does not identify individual vapors or measure all ultrafine particles.
